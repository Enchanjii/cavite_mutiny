Historical Context

The 1872 Cavite Mutiny

An uprising of Filipino military personnel and workers at the Spanish arsenal in Cavite on January 20, 1872. This watershed event served as a prelude to the Philippine Revolution, yet history recorded two starkly contrasting interpretations from the colonial rulers versus the native intellectuals.

Core conflict: Pre-planned Revolution vs. Localized Labor Strike.

Source A

The Spanish Account

José Montero y Vidal & Gov.-Gen. Rafael Izquierdo

  • Nature: Grand, pre-planned conspiracy and full-scale revolution to overthrow Spanish sovereignty.
  • Primary Causes: Instigation by native clergy, radical liberal books, and desires to establish a native kingdom.
  • Role of GOMBURZA: Masterminds and ringleaders who used religion to incite the native masses and troops.
  • Objective: Total expulsion of Spaniards and installation of a native "Hari".
Source B

The Filipino Account

Dr. Trinidad Hermenegildo Pardo de Tavera

  • Nature: Localized, spontaneous mutiny by disgruntled arsenal workers and soldiers.
  • Primary Causes: Sudden revocation of long-standing privileges (tribute and polo y servicio exemption) by Izquierdo.
  • Role of GOMBURZA: Innocent victims; framed by friars and the government to silence secular reformists.
  • Objective: A localized protest against administrative oppression rather than a separatist revolution.
